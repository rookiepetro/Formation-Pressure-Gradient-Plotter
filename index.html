User Manual: Formation Tester Pro
1. Overview

This tool plots Formation Pressure (X-axis) against True Vertical Depth (Y-axis, reversed). It is designed to identify fluid gradients, calculate fluid densities, and determine fluid contacts (e.g., Gas-Oil Contact, Oil-Water Contact).

2. How to Load Data
  • Prepare your data in Excel: Column A = Depth (m), Column B = Pressure (psi).
  • Copy both columns (do not include the header text).
  • Paste them into the "Paste Data" text box in the sidebar.
  • Click "Load Data". The points will immediately appear on the chart as black dots.
3. Drawing Gradients & Interpreting Fluids
  1. In the Gradient Analysis table, click the rows corresponding to a specific fluid leg (e.g., points in the Water Zone). Selected points will turn yellow on the plot.
  2. Set the Extension (Cycles) parameter.
    • A cycle of 1.0 draws the regression line exactly across your selected points.
    • A cycle of 2.0 or 3.0 extends the line further up and down the wellbore. This helps you visually find intersections with other fluid gradients.
  3. Type a Zone Name (e.g., "Water Leg").
  4. Labels: Use the "Show Density Labels on Plot" checkbox to toggle text boxes on/off.
  5. Click "Draw Gradient Line". A colored dashed line will appear.
4. How Fluid Density is Calculated (The Math)

The tool determines fluid density directly from the slope of the line you draw through your pressure points.

  • Step 1: The tool uses least-squares linear regression on your selected points to find the pressure gradient:
    $$Gradient = \frac{\Delta P}{\Delta TVD} \ \text{(in psi/m)}$$
  • Step 2: It converts the pressure gradient into fluid density. Because pure water (1.0 g/cc) exerts a hydrostatic pressure gradient of roughly 1.4223 psi/m, the tool applies this conversion constant:
    $$Density \ (\text{g/cc}) = \frac{Gradient}{1.4223}$$
  • Reference Values:
    • Gas: ~0.10 to 0.35 g/cc
    • Oil: ~0.60 to 0.90 g/cc
    • Water: ~1.00 to 1.15 g/cc
5. Advanced Controls
  • Zoom & Pan: Use your mouse wheel to zoom in on specific points. Hold Ctrl (or Command on Mac) and drag the chart to pan around. Click "Reset Zoom" to snap back.
  • Export: Click the "Save Plot Image" button to download a high-resolution PNG with a white background, ready to be pasted into your log reports.
  • Excess Pressure: Click the "Switch to Excess Pressure" link in the top header to use the Alton Brown method, which is superior for detecting very subtle pressure barriers and supercharging effects.
